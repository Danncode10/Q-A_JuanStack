## Understand it first

**The trap with "AI" startups:** judges have heard "we use AI" a hundred times. Saying "AI" isn't innovation. What's new is **what the AI is connected to** and **what we refuse to let it do.**

**Your three innovation points (know them like your name):**

| | What it means | Proof you can point to |
|---|---|---|
| **1. An assistant, not software** | Other apps add screens; owners still type, sort and file. With Juan you *say* it: "Invoice Reyes Corp ₱50,000." Juan prepares the invoice with a 2307 link, and you confirm. | Confirmation cards: nothing is saved until the owner confirms. Sparkle button explains any BIR term in plain words. |
| **2. AI you can trust with money** | The AI handles language. Fixed BIR rules handle math. AI can be wrong with words; it's never in charge of numbers. | Tax engine is separate code with automated tests. AI never sees invoices, TINs or computations. |
| **3. Compliance as a byproduct** | You don't "do taxes" at quarter end. Every invoice and payment you record already feeds the forms. | Paid invoice → transaction log → forms + SAWT/QAP attachments prepared. 2307 signed at payment. |

**Plus the platform idea:** one shared core with an industry system on top. Build BIR once; lawyers, contractors and property firms all get it.

**Where competitors stand:**

| Competitor | What they do well | What's missing | Our line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spreadsheets, paper, chat apps | Free, familiar | No tracking, no reminders, re-encode everything | "Nothing connects." |
| Odoo, Zoho, QuickBooks, Xero | Powerful | Built abroad; BIR needs setup or a consultant; made for accountants | "Built for accountants abroad; we're BIR-first and made for owners." |
| Taxumo, JuanTax | BIR-certified filing | Start at quarter end; no daily operations, no industry workflows | "They're where you file. We're everything before filing." |

## Questions

### 1. "What's innovative? AI chatbots are everywhere."
💡 The chatbot isn't the innovation; connecting it safely to the back office is.
🗣 "Most chatbots just talk. Juan does the paperwork: say 'invoice Reyes Corp fifty thousand,' and it prepares the invoice with a 2307 signing link, ready for you to confirm. And we made a rule other AI apps don't: the AI never does tax math. Fixed BIR rules do."
📌 Three pillars above. Jev routes each message to the right feature.

### 2. "Isn't this just an ERP with ChatGPT attached?"
💡 No: ERPs are built for accountants abroad; we're built for Filipino owners, BIR-first, and the AI is fenced in.
🗣 "An ERP gives you modules you have to set up and learn. JuanStack is built around BIR from day one: the 2307, the quarterly forms, the attachments. And the AI isn't bolted on: it can only prepare things the owner confirms, and it never touches the numbers."

### 3. "Who are your competitors?"
💡 Three groups; each solves one part.
🗣 "Three groups. Manual methods, spreadsheets and chat apps, with no tracking. Global software like Odoo and Zoho, which needs a consultant for BIR. And filing tools like Taxumo, which file returns but don't run daily operations. Each solves one part. We combine the industry system, BIR compliance and an assistant in one."
⛔ Never "we have no competitors."

### 4. "How are you different from Taxumo?"
💡 Taxumo = the end of the quarter. JuanStack = every day before it.
🗣 "Taxumo is where you file. JuanStack is everything before filing: invoices, collecting 2307s from clients, deadlines on your calendar, your industry system, and an assistant you can talk to. Taxumo starts at the end of the quarter; we work every day."
↪️ *"But they can file and you can't."* "True, and that's the next step for us. BIR certifies tax software per form, and Taxumo proved that path works. We'd follow the same one." (See file 02.)
⛔ Don't bash Taxumo. "Taxumo proved Filipinos will pay for tax software."

### 5. "What if Taxumo or a big company adds AI?"
💡 They'd add a chatbot to a filing tool; we own the daily data and the industry workflows.
🗣 "They'd add a chatbot to a filing tool. Our edge is where the data comes from: daily invoices, client 2307s, case files for lawyers, projects for contractors. And we're built for small Filipino businesses and Taglish from day one, not adapted later."

### 6. "What stops someone from copying you?"
💡 Code can be copied; trust, certified rules and users' history can't, quickly.
🗣 "Anyone can copy screens. What's hard to copy is CPA-certified tax rules, industry systems shaped by real interviews, and every quarter of a user's history already inside JuanStack. The longer they stay, the harder it is to leave."

### 7. "Why industry packs instead of one generic app?"
💡 Owners think in their trade's language; one core keeps it cheap to build.
🗣 "A lawyer thinks in cases and hearings; a contractor in projects and materials. A generic app makes them translate. Our packs change the words and features to fit the trade, but they share one core, so we build BIR once and every industry gets it."
📌 In the app, turning on AttyJuan changes the labels to Cases and Hearings and gives Juan legal-office rules (e.g. no legal advice).
⚠️ See file 08: the full case-management screens are still being brought in from the AttyJuan prototype.

### 8. "Why do you need AI at all? Is it a gimmick?"
💡 Owners don't have time to learn software; talking is faster.
🗣 "Small business owners don't have time to learn menus. Saying 'set a meeting tomorrow at 2' or 'how were sales this quarter?' is faster than clicking around. And the sparkle button explains BIR terms like ATC codes in plain Filipino-friendly words. No competitor does that."

### 9. "What's the hardest thing you built?"
💡 Turning BIR rules into correct code.
🗣 "Turning BIR rules into code: withholding rates, ATC codes, the quarterly forms and their attachment files. One wrong rule means a wrong return. That's why it has its own automated tests, and why we're paying a CPA to certify it."

## The 2307 story (the best real-world example you have)
> "When a company pays a lawyer, it withholds part of the fee as tax and should give a BIR Form 2307 as proof. Without that form, the lawyer can't claim the tax as a credit, so they effectively pay it twice. Clients forget all the time. In JuanStack, the client signs the 2307 online through a secure link that comes with the invoice, so the credit is never lost."
