## 2. How each file is built

Every question has the same parts. **Don't memorize the sample sentence.** Memorize the idea and the proof, then say it your way.

- 💡 **Idea:** the one thing your answer must get across. If you only remember this, you're fine.
- 🗣 **Say it:** one example of a good answer. Yours can sound different.
- 📌 **Proof:** facts and numbers that back it up. Use one, not all.
- ↪️ **If they push:** the follow-up a sharp judge asks next.
- ⛔ **Trap:** what not to say.

## 3. How a top team answers

### The 3-step answer: **Answer → Reason → Proof**
1. **Answer** in the first sentence. "Yes." "₱299." "Fifteen users." "Not yet."
2. **Reason** in one sentence. "Because…"
3. **Proof** in one sentence: a number, a real business, or something in the app.

Then **stop.** 20–30 seconds. Silence after a good answer is confidence, not a mistake.

> ❌ "So basically, um, our platform is kind of designed to help with, like, the BIR, and also…"
> ✅ "Fifteen paying users. Our fixed costs are about ₱4,000 a month, and fifteen Solo plans bring in ₱4,485."

### Bridge to a strength
End on something you're strong at. A weakness question becomes a plan question.
> "We don't have paying users yet. **That's deliberate:** we won't charge for tax work until a CPA certifies our rules, and that's the first thing this funding pays for."

### When you don't know
Never guess a number, and never freeze. Say what you *do* know.
> "I don't want to give you a wrong number. What I can tell you is…"

### When a judge doubts you ("students can't do this", "it's just ChatGPT")
Don't get defensive. Agree with the concern, then show proof.
> "That's a fair concern. That's exactly why we built it before pitching it: it's live at juanstack.com…"

### Handing off between members
Smooth hand-offs look like a real team.
> "I'll let [name] add the numbers." / "To add to that, from the technical side…"
Only add if it **adds** something. Two people saying the same thing wastes time.

### Body language
- Look at the judge who asked, then glance at the others.
- Pause one second before answering. It looks thoughtful.
- "Thank you, that's a good question" only for the hard ones. Not every time.
